Special edition

Declan Rice · the new contract

Rice, to 2031

Arsenal's best player of the last two seasons has, by every credible account, agreed to stay into his thirties. Nothing is signed yet. Here is what is actually known, what is only reported, why the club is doing it now, and what it means for the rest of the queue.

Updated 18 November 2026 · Arsenal have not announced anything yet. This page will flip when they do.

The agreement

Reported

Sky Sports News reported on Monday 16 November that Rice has verbally agreed terms. The Athletic's David Ornstein says it is a five-year deal running to the summer of 2031.

The signature

Expected soon

Rice himself said on England duty that a new deal was close and hopefully coming soon. Arteta called it a matter of time before the Liverpool game. Nobody has picked up a pen in public.

The money

Unconfirmed

Sky says the idea is to bring him in line with Bukayo Saka, the club's top earner. Weekly figures are doing the rounds. None is confirmed, so none appears here.

How he got here

  1. 2006 – 2013Seven years at Chelsea, then released at 14

    Joined Chelsea's academy aged seven and was let go at fourteen. West Ham took him on after a trial. Somewhere in Cobham there is a meeting nobody wants minuted.

  2. August 2017First-team debut, West Ham

    After a cameo at the end of 2016/17, his first proper start came at Southampton. He was 18 and a centre-back as often as a midfielder.

  3. May 2022Club captain at 23

    Took the armband from Mark Noble, which at West Ham is roughly like being handed the keys to the building.

  4. 7 June 2023Lifts the Conference League in Prague

    Captained West Ham to a 2-1 win over Fiorentina, their first major European trophy since 1965, and was named the competition's player of the season. Then left.

  5. 15 July 2023Arsenal, for a club record

    £100m up front, the most Arsenal had ever paid for anyone, at the time joint-most expensive English player there had been.

  6. 8 April 2025Two free-kicks past Real Madrid

    3-0 at the Emirates in the quarter-final first leg, and the first player to score two direct free-kicks in a Champions League knockout game. Real Madrid come back to the Emirates on 9 December. Nobody will mention it.

  7. June 2025Player of the Season, first time

    Ahead of Saka and Raya, after a season that started slowly and finished with him as the side's most important outfield player.

  8. May 2026Champions of England

    The first title in 22 years, confirmed on 19 May. 55 appearances, five goals and 11 assists across the season by Arseblog News's count. A Champions League final and a League Cup final were lost, which is the only reason anybody had anything to complain about.

  9. June 2026Player of the Season, again

    Only the sixth man to retain it, after Liam Brady, Ian Wright, Thierry Henry, Saka and Ødegaard. Not bad company for a lad Chelsea didn't fancy.

  10. 18 July 2026Captains England to third at the World Cup

    Scored after 134 seconds in the 6-4 third-place win over France. A bronze is not a trophy, but it is a medal, and he had lost two Euro finals.

  11. 21 October 2026Scores at the Allianz

    The equaliser in the 2-2 with Bayern, after Harry Kane had scored, which is the correct order for that particular sequence of events.

  12. 16 November 2026Says yes to five more years

    Sky reports the verbal agreement; Ornstein adds the 2031 end date. Raya, Timber and Hincapié reported to be next.

Before Arsenal: stories from the old clubs

Chelsea 2006 – 2013

Seven years, a best mate, and the door

He joined Chelsea's academy as a small boy and made a friend for life there, Mason Mount. They played together for six years. At fourteen, Chelsea let him go, reportedly over a lack of physicality, which looks funny now. His dad, who had seen how hard the game can be, hadn't been keen on football as a career in the first place. There were tears.

On the record

West Ham 2013

Released on Wednesday, training on Thursday

That same night Fulham invited him to train, and their academy was within walking distance of the family home in New Malden. The next day West Ham called. Tony Carr, the man who brought through Lampard, Ferdinand and Cole, said let's have a look. In Rice's words, he was released on the Wednesday night and training on the Astro at Chadwell Heath on the Thursday. He chose the long trip east, which meant changing schools and leaving his old life behind.

On the record

West Ham 2015

"Bambi on ice", and a 50-50 call

A growth spurt left him, in his own description, like Bambi on ice. At sixteen, West Ham's coaches were split down the middle on whether to keep him. Then he played centre-half for the Under-18s against Fulham, and academy chief Terry Westley gave him his scholarship. The rest of English football has had to deal with the consequences.

On the record

West Ham 2017 – 2023

Centre-back, midfielder, captain, Prague

His debut was the final minutes at Burnley in May 2017, under Slaven Bilić, who by most accounts didn't see much in him yet. His first season was largely at centre-back. Under David Moyes he moved into midfield, took the armband from Mark Noble, and in Prague in 2023 lifted the club's first European trophy in 58 years. Then, a month later, he left. Hard to call that leaving on bad terms.

Record

Ireland → England 2018 – 2019

Three caps in green

His paternal grandparents came from Douglas, in Cork, so he came through Ireland's youth teams and played three senior friendlies for them in 2018. Friendlies don't tie you to a country, so in 2019 he switched to England. It was a hard decision, and a very public one.

Record

Mount Still mates

The friendship that survived everything

Mount stayed at Chelsea and Rice didn't, and they have been on opposite sides of London derbies ever since. They still went on holiday together, ended up in England squads together, and Rice has joked that his partner gets jealous of the bromance.

Reported

Off the pitch

FamilyBorn on 14 January 1999 in Kingston upon Thames, south-west London, and grew up around New Malden. His dad Sean, who had seen how hard the game can be, was cautious about football as a career.

PartnerLauren Fryer, his childhood sweetheart, has been with him since well before he was famous. She has been by his side through all of it.

ReportedThey became parents in August 2022, when he was still at West Ham. He quietly confirmed it with a new tattoo. There's also a dog called Raffa, a Christmas present for Lauren one year.

Our readThe thread through all of it is a lad from Kingston who was told he wasn't good enough at fourteen and has spent every year since proving otherwise. Five more years at the club he chose fits that story.

Why this one matters

01It sets the price of the spine

Contracts at a big club are not individual deals, they are a pay scale. When Saka signed his extension, he became the line everyone else gets measured against. Sky's reporting is that Rice's deal brings him level with Saka, and that matters well beyond Rice: once your best midfielder and best forward sit on the same top band, every negotiation that follows (Raya, Timber, Hincapié, and eventually Ødegaard) is a conversation about where you sit below that band, not about how high it goes. It is the club drawing a ceiling and asking everyone else to fit under it.

That is also why the batching is not a coincidence. Rice was one of nine first-team players whose deals were due to run out in 2028. Leave them all and you have a summer of 2027 in which half the dressing room has eighteen months left and an agent with a phone. Sort the most important one first and the rest follow the template.

The question nobody is asking loudly enough: what happens with Ødegaard, whose contract ends at exactly the same time? Our captain special has the answer so far →

02Why now, and why both sides want it

On paper Arsenal were in no hurry. With a 2028 expiry and a club option for 2029, they could have kept Rice without a new deal for another two and a half years. So why pay up now? Because the option protects the club against losing him for nothing, but it does nothing about the thing that actually worries clubs: the player becoming unsettled while a richer suitor circles. A new deal at the top of the scale removes the grievance before it exists.

For Rice the logic is simpler. A five-year contract signed at 27 covers his whole peak and pays him like the club's best player. Signing now trades a little bargaining power (he could have held out until 2028 and gone to market) for security and recognition at the exact moment his value is highest. For a player who by every account is happy, that is an easy trade.

03The bit of the deal that costs money

Our honest read, and it is only our read: the first three years of this contract are a bargain and the last two are the price of the first three. Rice's game is built on covering ground, pressing, carrying the ball through midfield and delivering set pieces. The deliveries will age beautifully. The running is what goes first, and a 31- or 32-year-old box-to-box midfielder on top wages is the kind of thing that turns up on a squad-planning spreadsheet in red. Arsenal know that. Paying it is the cost of not losing him at 29, which would cost far more.

And if you want the version without nuance: the best player at the champions has agreed to stay for another five years. It is very good news.
